# Rise Above: A Journal for Finding Your Edge
### A companion for teen readers (ages 11–17)

This isn't a test. There are no right answers — just honest ones. Use these pages after each chapter, or all at once after finishing the book.

---

## Chapter 1–2: A Village by the River / The Challenge

*Tommy gets pushed aside by Ronny, and instead of staying quiet about it, he calls out what happened and challenges him — directly, not behind his back.*

1. Think of a time someone (a classmate, a teammate, a sibling) was careless or dismissive toward you. What did you actually do about it — and looking back, is there anything you'd do differently?
2. Tommy didn't try to out-speed Ronny. He just challenged him to show up and compete fairly. What does "competing fairly" look like for you, in something you care about right now?
3. Ronny underestimated Tommy because of how he looked. Where have you underestimated someone — or been underestimated yourself?

---

## Chapter 3: The Bitter Defeat

*Ronny loses, spends a sleepless night replaying it, and finally asks himself "why" until he reaches the real reason: overconfidence, not bad luck.*

1. Describe a recent loss, failure, or embarrassment — academic, social, in a sport or hobby. What story did you tell yourself about it in the first hour afterward?
2. Now do what Ronny did. Ask "why" at least three times in a row about that same failure, until you get past the surface excuse. What's underneath?
3. "Overconfidence is more dangerous than lack of skill." Where in your life might this be quietly true?

---

## Chapter 4: The Rematch

*Ronny apologizes, trains his focus instead of just his speed, and wins the rematch through real change — not luck.*

1. Is there someone you owe an honest apology to right now — not a quick "sorry," but the real kind Ronny gave? What's stopping you?
2. "Real growth is proven through action, not just intention." Think of one thing you've said you'd change about yourself. What's one action this week that would actually prove it?

---

## Chapter 5: Finding the Edge

*Tommy realizes he can't out-run Ronny on land, so he changes the terrain — racing through water, where his weakness becomes his strength.*

1. Make two honest lists: things you're naturally good at, and things you consistently struggle with. Don't be modest or harsh — just accurate.
2. "Never compete on your opponent's strongest terrain." Where in your life are you currently trying to win on someone else's terms — comparing yourself to a friend's grades, looks, followers, or talent — instead of playing to your own strengths?
3. What's one "river" in your life — a place where what looks like a weakness elsewhere could actually be your edge?

---

## Chapter 6–7: Smelling the Roses / Inventive Cycles

*Ronny slows down enough to actually notice his surroundings, gets an idea, and then improves it again and again by asking real people what they actually needed.*

1. When was the last time you sat still, without your phone, long enough to actually notice something new? What happened?
2. Ronny's first attempt at the raft failed. He didn't quit — he adjusted and tried again. Think of something you gave up on after the first failed attempt. What might happen if you tried it again, differently?
3. Before building anything, Ronny asked the villagers what they actually needed. Who could you ask, honestly, before assuming you already know what they want from you?

---

## Chapter 8: Water Under the Bridge

*Tommy and Ronny apologize, become real friends, and discover that racing as a team — each using their own strength — beats either of them racing alone.*

1. Who is a former rival, or someone you've had real tension with, that you could see becoming a genuine ally if the air were cleared?
2. "Teamwork allows strengths to shine and weaknesses to be covered." Think of a group project, team, or friendship where this was true — or where it broke down because nobody admitted their weak spot.

---

## Chapter 9: The Outcast

*Fiona gets rejected by team after team because of how she looks, and almost gives up — until two people who'd also been underestimated welcomed her in.*

1. Describe a time you felt like Fiona — new, different, or judged before anyone actually got to know you. What got you through it (or what would have helped)?
2. Have you ever been one of the animals who rejected someone too quickly, the way the kangaroo or the hippo did? What assumption were you making?
3. "One act of kindness can change everything." Who is someone in your world right now who might need exactly that from you?

---

## Chapter 10: Day of Reckoning

*Fiona starts the mountain leg obsessed with beating the goat ahead of her, flies badly because of it, then refocuses on her own goal and wins.*

1. Where in your life are you currently flying "their race" — measuring yourself against someone else's pace, grades, body, or social feed — instead of focusing on your own goal?
2. What happens to your performance, mood, or confidence specifically when you start comparing instead of focusing?
3. Write down one goal that's entirely yours — not measured against anyone else. What would "winning" at it actually look like?

---

## Chapter 11: Flying High

*Alone for the first time, Fiona almost lets the old mocking voices win — until she remembers "I am built for the sky," rises above the chaos, and wins by being exactly herself.*

1. What are the "mocking voices" in your own head — the specific sentences, not just the general feeling? Where did they come from?
2. Fiona didn't beat the chaos by fighting harder inside it — she rose above it and found a different vantage point entirely. What would "rising above" look like for a specific situation you're dealing with right now?
3. Finish this sentence, the way Fiona did: "I am not too _____________. I am built for _____________."

---

## Final Reflection: My Edge

After finishing the book, answer these on your own:

1. **My strengths** — list 3 things that are genuinely true about you, even if no one's said them out loud:
2. **My terrain** — where (which class, sport, friend group, creative pursuit) do those strengths actually get to show up?
3. **My pattern** — which character's struggle hit closest to home: Tommy's self-doubt, Ronny's overconfidence, or Fiona's rejection? Why?
4. **My next move** — one specific, doable thing you'll do in the next two weeks because of something in this book.

You don't have to be the fastest, the strongest, or the most liked.
You have to find your edge — and use it.
